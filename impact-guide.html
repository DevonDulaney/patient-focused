Cancellation Recovery
Preventable cancellations are the most expensive problem no one is measuring.
65%
reduction in surgical non-completion with pre-procedure video

Day-of surgical cancellations cost $2,000–$10,000 per case. National rates run 5–14%, and approximately 59.7% are preventable — NPO violations, missing labs, medication non-compliance, and patients who got scared the night before because they didn't understand what to expect.

In the largest private-practice study of its kind, pre-procedure video education delivered via text cut non-completion from 37.6% to 13.2% across 14 practices and 516 patients.

Consent Protection
The signed form proves a signature was obtained. It does not prove understanding.
114%
higher odds of payout when claims cite inadequate informed consent for procedures

In Candello’s database of approximately 500,000 MPL cases, claims citing inadequate informed consent for procedures had 114% higher odds of closing with payment (Adam Schaffer, MD, MPH, Candello Annual Community Summit, Sept 12, 2023).

In vascular surgery, 18.5% of active surgeons are named in a suit within any two-year window. 25% of venous and lymphatic disease claims specifically allege lack of informed consent. Only 14% of vascular patients meet the threshold for adequate consent comprehension after standard verbal discussion.

Search Visibility
Your patients are searching. The question is whether they find you or a stranger.
53×
more likely to reach Google page one with video content

86% of patients research providers online before booking. A systematic review of 3,221 vascular surgery YouTube videos found that 53% were rated poor quality — and the worst videos averaged 27,348 views, more than double the views of fair-quality content. Your patients are watching the worst material available.

Google classifies health content under its highest quality standards. A board-certified physician on camera is the strongest possible quality signal for health search results.

Time Recovery
The physician spends less time. The patient understands more.
6.7 min
saved per consent encounter with video education

Video-assisted consent reduced the consent conversation from 12.3 minutes to 5.6 minutes while simultaneously raising patient satisfaction from 65% to 86%. A separate study demonstrated a 33% reduction in physician time per consent encounter.

The physician doesn't spend less time because they're rushing. They spend less time because the patient arrives informed. The conversation shifts from education to Q&A — which is faster, more productive, and more satisfying for both sides.
